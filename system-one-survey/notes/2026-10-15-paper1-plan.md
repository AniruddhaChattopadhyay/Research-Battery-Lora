## 3. Scope of paper 1

**Working title:** "Same Question, Different Answer? A Controlled Study of System One Decision Models".

**Main claim (to test):** the published results disagree because the studies use different task types, perturbations, metrics and versions. Under one protocol, the disagreements become clear patterns.

| Experiment | Hypothesis | In paper 1 |
|---|---|---|
| E0 Access audit | — | Yes (done on 2026-10-15) |
| E2 Calibration | H1: calibration depends on the task type | Yes, main result |
| E3 Robustness | H2: the effect depends on the perturbation. H6: other questions in the same call change the answers. | Yes, main result |
| E5 Decision training | H4: decision training adds more to small backbones | Yes, main result |
| E6 Cascades | H5: a cascade keeps the accuracy of the System Two model at less than half of its cost | Yes |
| E7 Versions | H7: a new version changes a measurable part of the answers | Yes, short |
| E8 Latency | The published latencies differ because of the region, route and input length | Appendix |
| E1 Evidence synthesis | — | No: paper 2 (only a short related-work table in paper 1) |
| E4 Right tool | H3: a trained encoder is better above about 1,000 labels | No: paper 2 |

**Tasks:** the four tasks in `src/tasks.py` (BoolQ, Banking77, HelpSteer2, Civil Comments), plus When2Call, JudgeBench-Pro, Aegis 2.0 and one fresh task (arXiv papers from after 2026-10-01, with the primary category as the label).

**Baselines:** B1 (System Two models) on 200 items of each task, B2 (the open backbones with a label-likelihood readout) for E5, B3 (`gpt-6-luna` with structured outputs) and B4 (zero-shot encoders). B5 and the training of encoders move to paper 2.
