# QBranch: results of the second set of experiments (2026-11-09 to 2026-11-10)

Status: results for the meeting with Prof. Roy. Author: Aniruddha. The pilot is in
`notes/2026-11-09-pilot-log.md`. The raw outputs are in `runs/` (git ignores it). Code: `src/`, `analysis/`.

## 1. Summary for the meeting

1. **C2 (the world-model claim) holds on all four streams.** The open slots predict later facts better
   than chance. The effect is strongest on a dated news stream, where entities come back over time.
   Open *events* (a verdict, a signing) bind much more often than background facts.
2. **C1 (representation) holds on both full benchmarks for the graph, and on 2Wiki against dense.**
   The inferred-question index makes the graph better than a plain OpenIE graph (MuSiQue +3.6, 2Wiki
   +2.6 judge points, n = 1,000 each). On 2Wiki, the QBranch graph also beats dense retrieval (+3.5;
   +2.3 against dense with 10 passages), and with one reader call it equals the dense plan, which
   needs 4.6 calls. On MuSiQue, the graph does not beat dense retrieval at the same budget.
3. **The path-plan result of the 100-question slice did not replicate.** On the slice, the graph plan
   was +11 judge points over the dense plan. On 1,000 questions, it is −2.1. Planning helps dense
   retrieval as much as it helps the graph. The graph still helps on 4-hop questions (+6 judge, +7 F1).
4. **The symbolic anchor step hurts (−1.9).** The anchored facts push the passage for the next hop out
   of the evidence. The cause is clear, and so is the fix.
5. **The open questions do not hurt QA.** A build without them gives the same accuracy. So their value
   is in C2, at about 14% more build cost.

Recommendation: **submit to ICWM (2026-11-30) with C2 as the main claim.** The planning results are
negative so far, so the ICAPS angle (planning under a budget) has no result to build on yet. See
section 6 for the next experiments.

## 3. C2: do the open slots predict later facts?

For each sampled open slot (entity e, open question q, raised in batch t), the test collects the later
facts about e (batches after t, other passages). The checker (Claude Haiku 5.5) decides if one of the
30 nearest later facts answers q. The baseline asks the same about a random open question of another
entity of the same type, with the name of e put in. So both questions see the same entity and the same
later facts.

| Stream | Open slots | Ceiling (some later fact about e) | Answered, predicted q | Answered, random q | Difference | Already in memory |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| HotpotQA slice (5 batches) | 2,922 | 17.1% | 17.0% | 5.2% | +11.7 [+7.7, +15.7] | 2.4% |
| MuSiQue slice (5 batches) | 5,236 | 15.1% | 7.8% | 3.8% | +4.0 [+1.5, +6.3] | 2.1% |
| MuSiQue full (10 batches) | 34,849 | 20.2% | 11.5% | 6.2% | +5.3 [+2.5, +8.2] | 1.6% |
| **News, MultiHop-RAG (10 time batches)** | 14,181 | **31.5%** | **14.0%** | 5.0% | **+9.0 [+5.7, +12.3]** | **9.0%** |

"Answered" is the rate on a sample of 400–600 slots that have later facts. Over all slots, the rate is
the ceiling × this rate (news: 4.4% against 1.6% for random, 2.8×). "Already in memory" is over all slots.

Reading:
1. The predicted question is 2–3× more likely to be answered later than a random question about the same
   entity. The interval excludes zero on all four streams.
2. The news stream has the highest ceiling (31.5%), because entities come back over time.
3. On the news stream, 9% of the open slots were already answered in memory when the system opened them.
   The agent must check memory before it opens a slot.
4. The mean gap between the slot and its answer is 2–3 batches (news: about 3 weeks).

**Kinds of slots (news stream, 600 sampled slots).** 98% are background facts ("When was OpenAI
founded?"). Only 2% (11) are open events, but 5 of the 11 (45%) were answered later, and 0 of the
random questions were. The sample is small, but the build prompt asks for background facts now ("for
example the birthplace of a person..."). A prompt that also asks for open events is a clear next step.

|---|---|
| What is the outcome of the Epic v. Google trial? (The Verge, 2023-11-07) | Epic won (The Verge, 2023-12-12) |
| Which team will Shohei Ohtani sign with? (Sporting News, 2023-11-27) | Los Angeles Dodgers (The New York Times, 2023-12-09) |

Reading:
1. **The inferred questions make the graph better.** The question-driven graph with its question index
   beats the plain triple graph by 3.6 judge points. The graph without the index is equal to OpenIE, so
   the gain comes from the index of inferred questions.
2. **The graph does not beat dense retrieval at the same budget.** With 10 passages, dense gets 0.679
   and the QBranch graph gets 0.660.
3. **The planner helps, but it helps dense as much as the graph.** All plan systems are at 0.70–0.71.
   The planner costs about 4.6 reader calls and about 5 times more reader tokens per question.
4. **The graph helps on the longest chains.** On 4-hop questions, the QBranch plan is the best system
   (0.53). The gap to the oracle (0.75) is still large.

### 4.1 The 100-question slice did not predict the full run

| System | Slice, n = 100 (1,743 passages) | Full, n = 1,000 (11,656 passages) |
|---|---|---|
| dense | 0.70 | 0.632 |
| dense_k10 | 0.74 | 0.679 |
| dense_plan | 0.66 | 0.706 |
| graph_qbranch | 0.72 | 0.643 |
| graph_qbranch_plan | 0.77 | 0.685 |
| graph_qbranch_plan − dense_plan | **+11 [+3, +19]** | **−2.1 [−4.5, +0.3]** |

On the slice, the graph plan looked much better than the dense plan. On the full run, it is not. Two
reasons are possible: chance on 100 questions, and a smaller, cleaner graph on the slice (2,756 relation
names against 9,905, and fewer entities with the same name). Lesson: report claims only at n = 1,000.

### 4.2 Why the anchor step hurts

The anchor step keeps the 10 best facts of the entity that the earlier hop found, with a high score.
These facts also choose the passages. So the passages about the anchor entity take the slots of the
passage for the next entity. Example: "Which network created The Biggest Loser Brunei?" The anchor
"Brunei" fills the evidence with Radio Television Brunei facts, and the hop misses the NBC passage. In
39 of the 43 questions that the anchor broke, the anchor changed a hop answer. Fix: add the anchored
facts to the fact list, but do not let them choose the passages.

### 4.3 Other plan diagnostics

- The chains find the gold bridge entities equally often: 77–80% for all plan systems.
- The planner writes the gold number of steps for 78% of the questions.
- Routing by plan length (graph plan for long plans, dense plan for short ones, threshold chosen on the
  100 development questions) does not help on the other 900: −0.3 [−2.1, +1.6] against dense_plan.
- A run problem that I found and fixed: two processes ran the planner at the same time, so 89 of 1,000
  questions had different plans in different systems. I ran all plan systems again with one shared plan
  per question. The tables above use the shared plans. The first answers are in
  `runs/musique_nall/answers_mixed_plans/`.

Judge by question type: compositional (n = 413) dense 0.75, dense_k10 0.75, graph_qbranch 0.81,
dense_plan 0.84, oracle 0.84. The other types (comparison, bridge comparison, inference) are at
0.92–0.97 for all retrieval systems.

Reading:
1. **On 2Wiki, the QBranch graph beats dense retrieval**, also with 10 passages, and it is 2.2 points
   below the oracle. The gain is on compositional questions.
2. The pattern is the same as on MuSiQue: the gain comes from the inferred-question index, not from the
   graph alone (graph_qbranch_noq = graph_openie).
3. **Single-shot QBranch equals the dense plan** (0.897 against 0.902) with 1 reader call instead of 4.6
   and one third of the reader tokens. This is an accuracy-at-a-budget result.
4. The 2Wiki graph has 1,444 relation names (MuSiQue: 9,905). The 2Wiki questions come from a fixed set
   of Wikidata relations. The graph helps most when the schema is small and clean. This points to schema
   normalization as a main lever for MuSiQue.

## 6. Next experiments (proposal, in order)

1. **C2, prompt for open events.** Ask the build step for open outcomes and states (verdicts, signings,
   decisions) as well as background facts. Measure C2 by kind on the news stream. Cost: about $3.5.
2. **C2, check memory before a slot opens.** 9% of the news slots were already answered. Bind them at
   once and report the rate.
3. **Fix the anchor step** (anchored facts in the fact list only), and test only on full MuSiQue.
4. **C3, view management v1** (plan section 7). It is not started. It needs Prof. Roy's answer about
   what a view is (plan section 8, question 3).
5. **Document nodes** (source, date) in the graph, for news questions.
6. **FinanceBench** as a second stream with recurring entities (named in the kickoff meeting).

## 8. Limits

- One run per configuration (temperature 0, but the model output is not fully deterministic). The
  intervals cover the question sample, not the run-to-run variation.
- The judge and the C2 checker are one model (Claude Haiku 5.5). I did not check them by hand. A hand
  check of 50 C2 decisions is a good next step before the paper.
- The C2 baseline (a random open question of the same entity type) can make questions that do not fit
  the entity. This makes the baseline lower. A stricter baseline uses only the open questions that other
  passages raised about the same entity.
- HotpotQA was run only on the 100-question slice. The full HotpotQA run is still open (about $6).
